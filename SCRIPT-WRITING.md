# Script writing for H3 video: rules our node has actually measured

Source: the render node's writers' room (current to 2026-11-11). Generic: client names
and facts removed. Each rule below was paid for by a failed render or a measured comparison. Rules marked
(unmeasured) are not proven.

## Writing
1. **Photograph test, every line.** Use only verbs and objects you could photograph someone doing. Cut cliches ("spread like a virus").
2. **Swap test.** Put a competitor's name on the script. If it still works it is wallpaper; rewrite or add one element only this client has. Name any device or mascot as a device in the brief or the gate cannot see it.
3. **Facts come only from the brief/brand kit.** No invented percentages, no "up to", no fabricated before/after.
4. **One idea, fully committed.** More than two clever things: cut one.
5. **Brief is separate from script.** Facts + allowed claims + hooks in one doc; the read in another.
6. **Always write several options** (different mechanisms, not just wording) and let the human pick. Scoring one criterion per pass beat one blended pass (cross-criterion correlation 0.55 down to 0.08). Do not run critique-and-revise loops on taste; self-check only against ground truth (claims, banned words, budget).
7. **Distinctive asset in the first 2 s.** A logo open is the worst opening (-30%). **End on the emotional peak** (a person), then a very short card (about 4.5-5.3 s).
8. **Comedy scene must name its GAME and a reveal that is a second reading, not the premise restated.** Every scene states who wants what, what happens if they don't get it, and why now.
9. **Narrator is not a character.** Voice-over is whole spoken sentences to one person. Point-form ("Top pocket. Paper through the gate.") reads wrong. Dialogue between characters is the opposite: subtext, fragments.
10. **Write the VO after you have seen the footage.**
11. **Hook line first, then scenes**; every line tagged advances-story or supports-CTA.

## Producibility on MiniMax H3 (measured on our node)
- One event per take. Never two speakers, or an entrance plus an exit, in one generation.
- Words per second is a risk gradient, not a cliff: below 2.0 wps about 30% of takes get flagged (H3 drawls or invents words); 2.0-2.99 about 4%; 3.0+ 0% (small n). Clean single-talker clips exist down to 1.35 wps.
- Contractions by default (spelled-out reads robotic). Few sentence breaks, no ellipses inside a line.
- A silent character gets physical business only, never "says nothing".
- After recording, verify the audio against the script with speech recognition; the script is the truth, not the voice model.
- Keep a hand-written shot prompt out of the loop: compile it from the script fields so every take is traceable.
